# M2.25b decisions (probe scheduling on a dead volume)

Problem: a permanently dead volume could take every probe worker and, through priming's urgent re-queue, sit ahead of healthy files. First attempt (stall on one stuck probe, fail deferred files fast) was rejected in review: volumes are mounts (`/` for all local files), so one slow long file would have broken every unprobed file on the system disk.

Decisions (orchestrator, 2026-10-27):

- **No fail-fast.** Deferred files stay pending and keep any last answer; the 5 s hold limit and the stuck-probe rule apply to a GO unchanged. Why: a healthy disk must never make cues broken.
- **Corroborated stall only.** A volume is stalled for probes when the media watch reports it, or two different files' probes are stuck on it; preloads never count. Why: one slow file is not a dead mount, and the watch is the single source of truth (one notice).
- **One trial at a time** on a stalled volume, others wait without a worker; healthy volumes always proceed.
- **Abandoned jobs do not hold the volume**: after its timeout a stuck trial stops blocking the next (backoff 30 s doubling to 5 min); `reset()` clears volume state and ignores late reports of old jobs.
- **The system disk bucket `/` is never gated** (second review): it mixes the local disk with automounts and cloud paths, so a probe-side verdict there is meaningless; probes on `/` behave as on main.
- No pause of trials at some abandoned-worker count; the backoff bounds trials, and an answering trial clears the stall.
- Claims are keyed by (probe or preload, file).

Open: QLab 5's behaviour on a non-answering volume is undocumented to us; it does not mark media unsupported for a slow read, which this follows.
