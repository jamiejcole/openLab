# Patch slots, default mapping, set-mapping

## Slots (`Patches`, `control/patches/`)
- A patch's mixer matrix (slot) is allocated by `(DocId, PatchId)`, not by index in `Workspace::patches`, from one free list for all docs and devices (least recently freed first). Slot numbers are the same in every device's mixer; a slot's gains are sent to the mixer of its patch's device (`slot_output`), and a slot never sent to on a mixer is identity there. Every matrix is the mixers' cue outputs (128) by their device outputs (`Patches::new`); only entries that change are sent.
  A slot belongs to one doc's patch at a time. So inserting, removing, moving a patch (and undo/redo) is allowed while voices play and never re-routes or silences one.
- A voice keeps the slot its `VoiceStart` named until it ends, except that **a playing cue edited to name another patch follows it** (`Voices::follow_patches`, after every `sync_patches`): its voice (started, paused or stopping; not a preview) gets `MoveVoice` to the new slot, dated no earlier than its start/resume, and crossfades over the de-zipper.
  Outputs the new patch adds get their levels first (`Ramp`s, the model's with crosspoints, except a level a Fade cue set). No free slot for the new patch: the voice stays where it is until a later sync.
- The slot a voice left stays in use (`VoiceRec::former`) until the mixer reports every sent move settled (`MoveEnded.moves` equals the `MoveVoice`s sent) or the voice ends; a lost report only keeps the slot until then. `Voices::slots_in_use` lists slots of every voice the mixer may still hold (previews and orphans included).
- Rules applied on every `sync_patches`:
  - A patch keeps its slot while it exists. A new patch takes the least recently freed slot (at open, patch `i` gets slot `i`); none free means `unmapped` plus a `mixer_patches_exhausted` warning until one frees.
  - **A slot just handed to a patch takes its gains at once (`SnapPatch`)** instead of ramping from what it held, so a cue started or moved onto it that sample is heard only on its own patch's outputs.
  - A removed patch's slot is **retired** while a voice uses it: it keeps its routing and follows no other edit; when unused it is zeroed and freed (`reclaim_slots`, each tick while any is retired, then syncs again). An undo that restores the same id and output count takes the retired slot back. A slot is never handed out while a voice may play through it.
  - **Resize** (channel count or outputs) or **another device** under a playing voice: the voice keeps its layout (old slot retired) and the patch moves to a fresh slot, applying from the next start. With no voice on the slot the change is in place; a slot moving to another device is zeroed on the one it leaves. "Another device" counts the device the mapping names even while it cannot play yet (opening, unusable, gone): the voice's retired slot keeps its routing on the old device meanwhile.
  - A voice's own mixer never changes: `follow_patches` leaves a voice whose cue's new patch is on another device where it is; that voice moves by being replaced (`DocCx::follow_devices`, below).
- **Following the patch to another device** (AUD-07, skill `qlab-behaviour` Q450): after every `sync_patches` (and when a device is found unusable), `follow_devices` looks at every show voice that stands for its run (`Voices::sounding`). Its patch `Mapped` on a device whose output is not the voice's: `Input::DeviceMoved` to the core, which answers `RelocateVoice` (skill `cue-engine`).
  - Its patch's device listed and still opening: nothing yet (it plays on where it is; the open's sync moves it). Not listed, unusable (`Engine::unusable`) or `channels_missing` there: `ActionFailed { device_lost }` (shows paused, time frozen, as a lost device's cue; the end of its sound still playing in its post-wait is stopped as an orphaned tail), a `device_lost` notice and error log naming the device and why.
  - `Voices::relocate`: a playing voice plays on until `MOVE_LEAD` (0.1 s) after the move; a new voice starts on that sample on the new device's slot and the two crossfade over `SEEK_CROSSFADE`, as a seek's.
  - What the new voice plays is planned by one function for every plan, the first and each re-plan: `move_plan` takes the old voice's own state on that swap sample (its position, its table, the devamp cuts it was given and whether the latest stops it) and gives the rest of its table (`rest_of_table`): the rest of the pass it is in, the passes to come and what follows.
  - The plan's table is the old voice's own (at its device's rate, `MovePlan::rate`), cut where its sound is (the core's position converted by its `Scale`); its offset and length are master-clock frames. The new voice's stream is made at the new device's rate from that table rescaled (`openlab_audio::rescale`), and the swap and crossfade are dated on each device's own samples by `Outputs::route`. A move between devices at different rates lands in step to within about a frame.
  - A devamp cut still ahead ends the loop on its pass there and goes on after the slice (nothing for a stop), so the new voice leaves on the same sample with no devamp of its own; the core keeps its deadline. Nothing left by the swap (it ended, or will have): no move, the old voice keeps the run.
  - A devamp given while it waits to swap goes to the old voice, which is what sounds, so the loop is left at the end of the pass it came in. The swap waits for the answer (moved on by a lead, as for a late stream); the answer is the moved run's (`DevampScheduled`, `ActionLength`) and the move is planned again with that cut (`moved_devamp_answer`), or given up when the old voice has nothing left by the swap. A move paused before the answer takes it from the pause.
  - Its start is sent only once it is in the commit window, so a stop or pause before it reaches the old voice; a pause before the swap plans the move again from the pause. A paused voice is stopped at once and the new one waits for the resume. A voice not in a mixer yet only takes the fresh run.
  - Its `delay` starts at the core's count minus its own at the swap, so `ActionLength` is in the core's count and the end and auto-follow land on the moved sound's last sample.
  - Its stream not primed by the swap (a slow disk): the swap is planned again `MOVE_LEAD` on by `move_plan` (`replan_move`), the old voice playing on, so nothing plays twice.
  - Still not primed `SEEK_PRIME_WAIT` after the first planned swap, or nothing left to play by the new swap: the move is given up (`give_up_move`): the old voice keeps the run, with any devamp sent for the moved one, and the waiting record and stream go. Progress shows the old voice until the swap.
  - A move given up for a late stream is tried again (`VoiceRec::retry_at`, `Voices::take_due_moves` in the doc tick, then `follow_devices`) `MOVE_RETRY` (0.25 s) later, doubling after each give-up of the run up to 16 times; each try plans afresh with `move_plan`, so opens stay bounded and nothing plays twice. The first give-up of a run writes one `warn` line and a `move_delayed` notice.
  - `relocate` tells the core the run's length for the fresh run at once (the core's count plus what the old voice still has to play), so the end and an auto-follow keep their sample whether the move is made or given up.
  While the patch exists, every sync rebuilds the retired slot's first `min(old, new)` outputs from the current master, levels, mutes, polarity, crosspoints, device-output levels and device mapping (`Patches::retired_base`), so a mute or master pull-down still reaches the playing cue; outputs the patch no longer has keep their last gains. With no voice on the slot the resize is in place. This is openLab's rule (skill `qlab-behaviour` Q226).
  - Level, mute, polarity, master, crosspoint, device-output and mapping edits apply in place, so to voices already playing (`SetPatchRouting`, over the de-zipper).

## Matrix gains (`fill_routing`)
- Gain of cue output `o` to device output `d` = patch master x cue output level (mute: 0, polarity: negative) x crosspoint x device output `d`'s level (mute: 0).
- The crosspoint is the patch's listed one for `(o, d)` if any (`-120` dB: silent); else 1.0 where `d` is the device channel the mapping gives `o`; else 0. So a patch with no crosspoints routes exactly as its mapping (one device output per cue output).
- Device outputs at or past the slot's mixer width are never sent and never written into the next row. A mapped patch whose live crosspoints (above silence, for a cue output it has) or device-output entries name such outputs is kept as it is and reported: `Patches::routing_beyond` and the `patch_routing_beyond_device` device notice (skill `qlab-behaviour` Q510). It does not break the patch or its cues.
- A closed doc's slots are zeroed and freed at once; a doc whose patches waited for a slot syncs on the next wake.

## Default mapping
Before each patch resolution (`sync_patches`: start, doc opened, edits like AddPatch, device back), every patch with **no mapping entry** on this machine gets, when the primary device plays, a mapping of its cue outputs 1..N to device outputs 1..N in `config.machine`, N at most the device's outputs (skill `qlab-behaviour` Q515). The settings are written by the settings writer (a failed write is a `warn`, not the control thread) with an `info` log.
- **A patch with an entry is never touched** (an operator's mapping is never overwritten).
- A device with fewer outputs than the patch is mapped on the outputs it has: the mapping lists channels for the patch's first outputs only, the rest have no default route (crosspoints may still send them anywhere). The patch plays and its cues are not broken. A device with no outputs leaves it unmapped, one `warn` per patch and device.
- A mapping lists channels for the patch's first outputs (1 to its count; `resolve_patch` is `output_count_mismatch` only for more). A short entry is not grown by a device sync (two open docs of one workspace id must not refit each other): reopening the show on a bigger device fits it.
- No playable device (outage, none listed): nothing is written; patches stay `Unmapped` until a sync with a device.
- A patch the operator cleared has an explicit empty entry, so it counts as having an entry.

## Fitting a mapping to its patch (`fit_mappings_to_patches`)
- When: on this doc's opening and after each of its edits, undo and redo (`sync_workspace`, not a device sync, so two open docs of one workspace id never refit each other).
- An entry whose channel count differs from its patch's outputs is fitted: kept outputs keep their channels, a new output `n` goes to channel `n` while that is one of the usable outputs (the device's, at most the primary's count; a device not listed: the channels the entry already reaches), and it and later ones get none past them; removed ones drop.
- Written like the default mapping, `info` log. A cleared entry is left alone. So a cue-output count edit is one request and never leaves the patch `output_count_mismatch` (skill `qlab-behaviour` Q281).

## `Request::SetPatchMapping { patch, mapping? }` (`Control::set_patch_mapping`)
Wire shape and errors: `docs/protocol.md`.
- Validated first against `list_devices` (device exists; one channel per patch output; each below the device's output count) and the doc's patches; a refusal (`not_found`, `bad_request`) changes and writes nothing. A mapping on the system output (`system`, no device name) is checked against the device marked default; none: `not_found`.
- Machine settings are engine-wide, so the entry is keyed by **workspace id and patch id**, as the default mapping stores it (two open docs with one workspace id share it). Written like the default mapping.
- `mapping` null **clears**: entry becomes `{"device": "", "channels": []}` (patch `Unmapped`, default mapping never re-applies). A clear first **pauses every Audio cue playing (running or fading) through that patch** in each open doc with that workspace id (`DocCx::pause_cues_on`, an ordinary Pause, as QLab 5.5.3 does when a cue's device is set to none); mapping a device again does not resume them. Then every open doc re-resolves patches (`sync_patches`).
- A mapping to other channels of the same device moves a playing cue to the new outputs in place (the slot follows the mapping). A mapping to another device moves a playing cue there at once, in step ("Following the patch to another device" above; skill `qlab-behaviour` Q450); one it cannot play on stops as on a lost device. Idle cues show broken or playable from the new resolution.
- A mapping to any device `list_devices` names is accepted. One the engine does not play on yet opens for it (skill `devices`, several devices); the patch is `device_missing` until it plays.
- A change that cannot be kept (settings folder configured but `settings.json` unreadable at start, so the engine leaves the file alone; or the settings writer not running) is refused with `io` and changes nothing. A later disk write failure is only a `warn`.
